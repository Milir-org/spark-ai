Redesign the PPC / Paid Search campaign setup flow to be simple, guided, and suitable for solopreneurs and small teams who may not be PPC experts.

Important product model:
A user creates one SPARK Paid Search Campaign. That campaign can contain multiple platform-specific campaign plans for Google Ads, Microsoft Advertising/Bing, Baidu, Naver, and Yahoo Japan placeholder.

Do not make the user feel like they are manually creating separate campaigns for every platform. The workflow should feel like:
“One campaign goal → choose platforms → SPARK creates platform-specific plans.”

Main PPC Workbench layout:
At the top, show three large action cards:
1. Guided Campaign Wizard
Description: Let SPARK guide you from business goal to paid search campaign plan.
Button: Start Guided Campaign

2. Advanced Campaign Setup
Description: Build a search campaign with full control over keywords, bids, ads, and tracking.
Button: Create Advanced Campaign

3. Import & Optimise Existing Campaigns
Description: Connect your search ad account and let SPARK find what to fix, pause, scale, or rewrite.
Button: Import Campaigns

Below these cards, show Existing Search Campaigns table with:
- Campaign name
- Platforms
- Primary goal
- Status
- Budget
- Spend
- Avg CPC
- Conversions
- Cost per lead
- Tracking status
- SPARK recommendation
- Approval status
- Actions

Actions:
- View
- Edit
- Optimise
- Duplicate
- Request Approval

Now build the Guided Campaign Wizard.

Wizard layout:
Use a clean two-column layout.
Left side: campaign setup form.
Right side: persistent SPARK Brain panel.
Top: progress indicator.

Wizard steps:
1. Goal
2. Offer & Audience
3. Platforms & Budget
4. Keywords & Search Intent
5. Ads, Tracking & Campaign Pack
6. Review & Approval

Step 1: Goal
Ask:
- Primary goal: Leads, Sales, Bookings, Website Traffic, Brand Protection, Competitor Conquest, Local Enquiries
- Secondary goals: allow multiple selection
- Guardrails: max CPL, max daily spend, do not launch without tracking, location restrictions
- Campaign urgency: Test, Normal, Aggressive

Rule:
Only one primary goal is allowed. Secondary goals can be multiple. SPARK Brain should explain that the primary goal drives optimisation while secondary goals are tracked.

Step 2: Offer & Audience
Ask:
- Product or service being promoted
- Offer or call-to-action
- Target geography
- Target customer type
- Landing page URL
- Exclusions or restrictions

SPARK Brain should summarise the audience and warn if the offer is vague.

Step 3: Platforms & Budget
Ask:
- Platform selection mode: Let SPARK recommend platforms / I will choose manually
- Platforms: Google Ads, Microsoft Advertising/Bing, Baidu, Naver, Yahoo Japan placeholder
- Total budget
- Campaign duration
- Daily budget
- Budget style: Conservative, Balanced, Aggressive

SPARK Brain should generate recommended budget allocation across selected platforms.

Example:
Google Ads 70%
Microsoft/Bing 20%
Naver 10%
Baidu not recommended unless targeting China traffic

Show platform cards with:
- platform name
- recommended budget
- estimated CPC/bid forecast range
- recommended campaign template
- tracking status
- readiness status

Do not call this real-time exact bid pricing. Use “estimated CPC / bid forecast”.

Step 4: Keywords & Search Intent
Ask:
- What would customers search for?
- Brand keywords
- Service/product keywords
- Competitor keywords
- Location keywords
- Negative keywords

SPARK Brain should generate:
- keyword groups
- high-intent keywords
- suggested match types
- negative keyword suggestions
- platform-specific keyword notes

Step 5: Ads, Tracking & Campaign Pack
Generate campaign assets based on goal, audience, platforms, keywords, landing page and brand rules.

Show:
- ad headlines
- ad descriptions
- sitelinks
- callouts
- structured snippets placeholder
- CTA suggestions
- final URL
- UTM structure
- GA4 conversion event
- CRM lead source mapping
- tracking checklist
- launch readiness warnings

Ad copy generation should happen only here, not as a top-level page button.

Step 6: Review & Approval
Show final SPARK Paid Search Campaign Plan.

Sections:
- campaign summary
- primary and secondary goals
- guardrails
- selected platforms
- platform-specific campaign cards
- budget allocation
- keyword plan
- ad copy
- landing page and tracking checklist
- risk warnings
- approval requirements
- launch readiness score

Approval checklist:
- budget approval
- keyword approval
- ad copy approval
- tracking approval
- launch approval

Buttons:
- Save Draft
- Request Approval
- Create Platform Drafts
- Simulate Launch
- Simulate Sync to Search Platforms

SPARK Brain behaviour:
The SPARK Brain panel should appear throughout the wizard and update based on the current step.

It should show:
- summary of current choices
- AI recommendations
- missing information
- risk warnings
- suggested next action

Use realistic mock AI outputs. The app does not need real platform integration yet.

Important UX rule:
The wizard should not feel like Google Ads. It should feel like SPARK is guiding a non-expert user through the campaign setup intelligently.

Do not overload the wizard with advanced PPC fields. Put advanced controls under expandable “Advanced Options”.
